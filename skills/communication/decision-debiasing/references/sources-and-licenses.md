# Sources and Licenses

## Scope of these references

The methods in this skill are well-established decision-science and
decision-engineering techniques, summarized and adapted here as a reusable prompt
protocol. No copyrighted text is reproduced verbatim; the scripts, tables, and
worked examples are original compositions provided as subject matter.

## Primary methodologies

- **Gary Klein — pre-mortem.** Klein, G. (2007). *Performing a Project Premortem.*
  Harvard Business Review. The pre-mortem technique: prospective hindsight ("it has
  already failed") to surface risks that conventional "what could go wrong" reviews
  suppress. The past-tense framing and the 10-15 reasons procedure follow his
  description.
- **Howard & Abbas — decision quality.** Ronald A. Howard and Abbas, A. E. (2015),
  *Foundations of Decision Analysis.* The decision-quality framing — clear frame,
  alternatives, preferences, information, and logic — informs the workflow
  structure and the decision record.
- **Daniel Kahneman — Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011).** The catalog of cognitive
  biases (anchoring, availability, framing, overconfidence, sunk cost, etc.) and
  the distinction between System 1 and System 2 underpin the bias-buster
  checklist.
- **Wikipedia — List of cognitive biases.** The bias catalog draws on the
  community-curated list, available under the Creative Commons Attribution-
  ShareAlike (CC BY-SA) 4.0 license. Where a bias definition reflects that source,
  it is summarized, not quoted; per CC BY-SA, adaptations carry the same share-alike
  spirit.

## Attribution notes

- This skill does not predict outcomes. The methods improve the quality of the
  thinking process (frame, alternatives, scrutiny, record) but cannot remove
  uncertainty. A debiased decision can still lose; an intuitive decision can still
  win.
- Where a bias "example" is given, it is illustrative, not a claim about any
  specific person or company.
- Professional decisions (financial, legal, medical) are out of scope for any
  recommendation; this skill provides thinking structure only and routes those
  calls to licensed experts.
