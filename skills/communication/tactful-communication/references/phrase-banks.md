# Phrase Banks — Four Core Scenarios

> These banks hold Chinese scripts as **subject matter**, the way a grammar
> reference holds example sentences. Each entry: formula description (English),
> example scripts (Chinese + English gloss), what NOT to say, and register
> variants. All scripts must be used honestly — never to fake commitment or
> string someone along.

## 1. Decline (拒绝) — the sandwich

**Formula:** affirmation of the request/relationship + honest reason + an
alternative or softened door. Never a bare "no".

**Examples**

- *Declining extra work from a colleague (casual):*
  「这个事我很想帮你，不过我这周手上两个截止日期都压着，实在抽不出整块时间。要不我把之前整理的模板发你，你先照着搭，卡住了我中午帮你看十分钟？」
  *Gloss:* "I'd really like to help, but I'm up against two deadlines this week and can't carve out a solid block. How about I send you the template I used, and we can spend ten minutes at lunch if you get stuck?"

- *Declining a boss's unreasonable request (formal, pushback framing):*
  「领导，我明白这个事的重要性。我目前手上是 A 和 B 两个优先级任务，如果再接 C，三个并行我担心质量和进度都保不住。建议先聚焦 A 和 C，B 往后排，优先级您来定？」
  *Gloss:* "I understand how important this is. Right now I own A and B as priorities; if I take on C as well, I'm worried neither quality nor schedule holds across all three. I suggest focusing on A and C and pushing B back — could you set the priority?"

- *Declining a favor / loan (close):*
  「咱们这关系我不跟你客套。这笔钱数我最近真腾不出来，硬借了你我自己也周转不开。要不我先帮你问一圈有没有更合适的渠道？」
  *Gloss:* "Between us I'll be straight with you. I genuinely can't free up that amount right now; if I force it I'll be the one in trouble. Let me ask around for you for a better channel instead."

**What NOT to say (bad AI output)**

- 「这个任务无法完成。」 ("This task cannot be completed.") — bare, blunt, no face saved.
- 「抱歉，因客观条件限制，本请求暂不具备可执行性，敬请谅解。」 — stiff, bureaucratic, sounds like a bot.
- Ghosting / total silence, or 「再说吧」used deceptively to delay a real answer.

**Register variants:** formal = full honorifics + priority table; casual = direct but warm; close = drop the honorifics, be plain, still offer help.

## 2. Comfort (安慰) — observe + acknowledge + presence, NO advice

**Formula:** name the emotion you observe + acknowledge it is real + offer presence.
**Explicitly BANNED phrases:** 别难过 / 想开点 / 这有什么大不了的 / 我早就跟你说过 / 别哭了没事的. These minimize the feeling and shut the person down.

**Examples**

- *Comforting a grieving friend (close):*
  「听到这个消息我也不知道该说什么。你现在肯定特别难受吧，想说说就说，不想说我就陪你坐一会儿。」
  *Gloss:* "I don't quite know what to say hearing this. You must be in so much pain right now. If you want to talk I'm here; if not, I'll just sit with you."
  *(Note: no advice, no "everything happens for a reason", no quick fix.)*

- *Comforting a colleague who failed a pitch (casual):*
  「这次准备了你那么久，结果没成，换谁都会失落。要不要下楼喝杯东西？不聊工作也行。」
  *Gloss:* "You put so long into this and it didn't land — anyone would feel let down. Want to head downstairs for a drink? We don't have to talk work."

**What NOT to say (bad AI output)**

- 「别难过了，没事的，想开点。」 ("Don't be sad, it's fine, just look on the bright side.")
- 「这有什么大不了的，下次再来嘛。」
- 「我早就跟你说过应该……」 (starts advice + blame)

**Rule:** presence before fixing. Only offer advice if the person explicitly asks "你说我该怎么办？".

## 3. Apology (道歉) — specific behavior + impact + remedy

**Formula:** name the exact behavior + state its impact on the other person +
concrete remedy / prevention. Vague 「对不起」without specifics reads as empty.

**Examples**

- *Missing a deadline (formal):*
  「上周三答应交付的方案我晚了两天，这让你们周五的评审排期被打乱，是我的问题。我已经把终稿发你了，后续我会提前两天设一个自查节点，避免再出现这种情况。」
  *Gloss:* "The proposal I promised last Wednesday is two days late; this pushed your Friday review off schedule, and that's on me. The final draft is sent; going forward I'll add a self-check node two days ahead so this doesn't happen again."

- *Forgetting an appointment (close):*
  「昨晚把饭局忘了，让你一个人等了我半小时，真的很不好意思。今晚我请，你挑地方，下次我一定提前一小时设提醒。」
  *Gloss:* "I completely forgot last night's dinner and left you waiting half an hour — that's on me, sorry. It's my treat tonight, you pick the place, and I'll set an hour-ahead reminder next time."

**What NOT to say**

- 「对不起啊，不好意思哈。」 with no specifics and no remedy.
- 「抱歉，但当时情况是……」 (lead with excuse; it cancels the apology).
- Over-apologizing to the point of making the other person comfort *you*.

## 4. Criticism / Feedback (批评/反馈) — behavior not person + joint solving + blocker inquiry

**Formula:** talk about the observable behavior and its impact, never label the
person; frame it as a shared problem; then ask what is blocking them.

**Translation table (blunt label -> face-preserving version)**

| Blunt / AI-like (do NOT say) | Face-preserving version (say this) |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| 你不行 / 你能力不行 | 「这个任务对你可能挑战有点大，我们一起拆解一下？」 |
| 你效率太低了 | 「最近进度是不是有点紧？是不是遇到什么卡点了？」 |
| 你怎么听不懂 | 「可能是我没讲清楚，我换个方式再说一遍。」 |
| 你怎么又搞错了 | 「这两处和咱们对的口径不太一样，我们一起看下是哪里岔了。」 |

**Example (peer feedback, casual):**
「你这次交的数据里有两列和上次对不上，报表那边对了半天。是不是最近数据源换了你没同步？我们一起把口径对一下，省得后面再返工。」
*Gloss:* "Two columns in your data don't match last time and the reporting side spent ages reconciling. Did the source change and not get synced? Let's align the definitions together so we don't have to redo it again."

**What NOT to say:** person-level labels (你就是… / 你总是… / 你怎么这么…), public correction in front of others, or sarcasm.

## 5. Asking for a raise / promotion (谈薪/晋升)

**Formula:** prepare evidence first → frame as value delivered (not need) → propose
a specific, anchored number/title → handle the soft "we'll consider it" honestly.
Never lead with "I need the money" or "everyone else gets paid more."

**Examples**

- *Requesting a raise (formal, value-framed):*
  「想跟您约个时间聊一下我的发展。过去这一年我独立扛下了 X 项目，把交付周期从两个月压到了三周，同时带了两个新人。基于这些产出，我希望薪资能调整到 [具体数字] 这个区间，您看这个方向可行吗？」
  *Gloss:* "I'd like to set some time to talk about my growth. Over the past year I owned the X project, cut delivery from two months to three weeks, and onboarded two juniors. Based on that output, I'd like to move my salary into the [specific number] range — does that direction feel workable to you?"

- *Requesting promotion (formal, evidence list):*
  「我想申请晋升到下一级。我整理了一份材料，列了我在独立负责项目、跨团队推动和带人这三块达成的结果。想请您看看，差距具体在哪几项，我好针对性补。」
  *Gloss:* "I'd like to apply for the next level. I put together a document listing my results on independently owning projects, cross-team driving, and mentoring. I'd like your read on which specific gaps I still need to close, so I can target them."

- *Handling "we'll consider it" (casual, read the subtext):*
  「明白，公司这边有流程。那我们定个时间，比如一个月后，再对一次进展和结果，您看可以吗？」
  *Gloss:* "Understood, there's a process on the company side. Can we set a time — say a month out — to review the progress and results again?"
  *(Note: "我们会考虑的" usually means no right now. Do not argue the no; convert it to a concrete follow-up date and measurable criteria instead.)*

**What NOT to say**

- 「我房租涨了 / 我要养家，能不能给我加点工资？」 ("My rent went up / I have a family, can you pay me more?") — need-based, not value-based; it is not the company's problem.
- 「某某跳槽涨了 50%，我也要。」 — compares the person, not the contribution.
- Threatening to quit unless you get it, or springing the ask as an ultimatum on the spot.

## 6. Declining a social invitation (婉拒社交)

**Formula:** thank them for asking + a short honest reason + offer an alternative
plan. Keep the reason brief — over-explaining sounds like you are lying.

**Examples**

- *Declining a dinner invite (casual):*
  「谢谢想着我！今晚我确实有安排了，走不开。要不这周末我请你喝东西？我们好好聊。」
  *Gloss:* "Thanks for thinking of me! I really have a commitment tonight and can't get away. How about I treat you for drinks this weekend instead, and we can properly catch up?"

- *Declining a weekend gathering (casual):*
  「这次就不凑啦，我周末想在家歇一天充个电。下次你们组局我一定到，提前喊我。」
  *Gloss:* "I'll sit this one out — I want to stay home and recharge this weekend. Next time you guys organize something, definitely count me in; give me a heads-up early."

- *Declining a formal event you cannot attend (formal):*
  「非常感谢邀请，很荣幸。那天我人在外地赶不回去，实在参加不了，提前祝活动顺利，下次一定到场。」
  *Gloss:* "Thank you very much for the kind invite, it's an honor. I'll be out of town that day and genuinely cannot make it; wishing the event a great success in advance, and I'll be there next time."

**What NOT to say**

- A long, detailed, escalating excuse with invented specifics ("本来可以去，但是我妈突然……我朋友……然后猫又……") — the more detail, the faker it sounds.
- A flat 「不去。」 ("Not going.") — no thanks, no door left open.
- Ghosting the invite or letting it hang with no answer.

**Rule:** one compact reason + one alternative. Extra detail = suspicion.

## 7. Giving feedback to a peer (同级反馈)

**Formula:** observable observation + its impact + a request. No judgment, no
person-label. Because a peer has no authority over you, joint problem-solving lands
far better than correction.

**Formula in one line:** 「我注意到 X 发生了，这造成了 Y，我们能不能聊聊 Z？」
("I noticed X happened, it caused Y, can we discuss Z?")

**Examples**

- *Peer feedback (casual):*
  「我注意到这次上线你没有同步测试组，结果他们周一才拿到版本，临时加了个班。下次咱们能不能提前一天拉个群同步？省得大家被动。」
  *Gloss:* "I noticed the test team wasn't looped in on this launch, so they only got the build Monday and had to put in last-minute overtime. Next time, could we pull them into a group one day ahead? It saves everyone from being on the back foot."

- *Peer feedback on a shared deliverable (casual):*
  「咱们那版方案里数据口径和上次对不上，我自己来回核对花了挺久。是不是我们下次开工前先花十分钟把口径定下来，后面就不用返工了？」
  *Gloss:* "In our version, the data definitions didn't line up with last time, and I spent a while reconciling them back and forth. Could we spend ten minutes locking the definitions up front next time, so we don't have to redo it?"

**What NOT to say**

- 「你总是这样 / 你怎么老不通知别人。」 ("You always do this / why do you never loop people in.") — person-label, triggers defensiveness.
- 「你这个做得不对。」 — judgment with no observation, impact, or ask.
- Raising it in front of others, or copying the boss on a peer-level correction.

## 8. Receiving criticism (接受批评)

**Formula:** do NOT defend immediately → ask for specific examples → summarize what
you heard → commit to one concrete change. The instinct to explain away the point
first is what turns feedback into a fight.

**Examples**

- *Receiving feedback from your boss (formal):*
  「谢谢您直接跟我说，这个对我挺重要。能麻烦您举一两个具体的例子吗？我想准确理解是哪件事、哪个环节。……我复述一下：您是说我在跨部门对齐上不够主动，对吧？那我接下来先从每次项目启动前发一封对齐邮件做起。」
  *Gloss:* "Thanks for telling me directly; that matters to me. Could you give one or two concrete examples? I want to pin down which thing, which step. ... Let me play it back: you mean I'm not proactive enough on cross-team alignment, right? Then to start, I'll send an alignment email before each project kickoff."

- *Receiving critical peer feedback (casual):*
  「我懂你意思了，不是说我态度有问题，是我那个结论给得太快、没带你看过程。我记住了，下次我把中间步骤一起发你，咱们对一下再下结论。」
  *Gloss:* "I get what you mean now — it's not that my attitude was off, it's that I landed on the conclusion too fast without walking you through the process. Got it; next time I'll send you the intermediate steps and we'll align before concluding."

**What NOT to say**

- 「不是的，当时其实是因为……」 — defending and explaining before you even understand the feedback.
- 「行，我知道了。」 ("OK, I got it.") — brushed off, signals you're not actually taking it in.
- Counter-attacking the critic ("你自己上次不也……").

**Rule:** absorb first, rebut later. Ask for specifics; the specific example is
where the real lesson lives.

## 9. Networking / cold outreach (社交破冰)

**Formula:** find common ground → give a specific, genuine compliment → make a
low-commitment ask. Do NOT lead with a big ask (a job, a favor, a referral) in the
first message; you owe the relationship before you cash it.

**Examples**

- *Cold outreach to a person you admire (formal, short):*
  「您好，我是 [公司/方向] 的 [名字]。读了您那篇关于 [具体文章/观点] 的分享，里面 [具体某一点] 对我正在做的事特别有启发。冒昧打扰，不知道您是否方便在下周喝杯咖啡聊二十分钟？时间和地点都听您的。」
  *Gloss:* "Hi, I'm [name] from [company/field]. I read your piece on [specific article/point], and [this specific point] was genuinely useful for what I'm working on. Sorry to cold-message you — would you be open to a 20-minute coffee sometime next week? Totally flexible on time and place."

- *Following up at an event (casual):*
  「刚会上聊得挺投机，你提到的 [具体话题] 我回头也想再深入看看。这是我微信，方便的话加一下，以后有相关的事咱们多交流。」
  *Gloss:* "That was a great chat in the session; the [specific topic] you mentioned is something I want to dig into more. Here's my WeChat — let's connect if it's convenient, and compare notes on related things down the line."

- *Asking for an intro without overreaching (casual):*
  「我注意到你和 [某人] 是一个圈子的。不急，哪天你们俩方便、气氛也合适的时候，帮忙提一句我就行，不用正式引荐。」
  *Gloss:* "I noticed you're in the same circle as [them]. No rush — whenever it's natural and the moment feels right, just mentioning me would be enough; no need for a formal intro."

**What NOT to say**

- Lead with the ask: 「你好，能不能帮我内推个工作？」 ("Hi, can you refer me for a job?") on a first message to a stranger.
- A generic compliment: 「你很优秀 / 久仰大名。」 — vague, flattery with no signal you actually read their work.
- Asking for an open-ended big favor up front, or demanding an immediate reply.

**Rule:** common ground first, specific compliment second, small ask third. The big
ask comes later, after value has been given.
