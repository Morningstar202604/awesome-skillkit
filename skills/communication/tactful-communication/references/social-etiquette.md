# Social Etiquette Reference

Compact reference for money amounts, gift taboos, banquet seating, and the
bribery line. Amounts are common baseline norms in mainland China; adjust for
city, era, and your own closeness.

## 1. Gift money (随份子) — common baseline amounts

| Relationship | Typical amount |
|--------------|----------------|
| Colleague / distant acquaintance | ¥200 |
| Classmate / regular friend | ¥500 |
| Close friend | ¥600 – ¥1000 |
| Best friend | ¥1000+ |
| Sibling / very close family | ¥2000+ |

**Number rules:**
- Celebrations (wedding, birthday): give **even** numbers, prefer 6 (顺), 8 (发), 9 (久). **Avoid 4** (sounds like death).
- Funerals: give **odd** numbers, use white or blue paper, **no red envelope**.
- Put it in a red envelope (红包) with your name on the flap.

## 2. Gift taboos (homophone / unlucky meanings)

| Gift | Why it is taboo | Safe swap |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| Clock (送钟) | sounds like 送终 = attending a funeral | flowers, fruit basket |
| Umbrella (伞) | sounds like 散 = split up / break up | something durable |
| Pear (梨) | sounds like 离 = separation | apples, oranges |
| Shoes (鞋) | implies the recipient walks away | clothing that is not footwear |
| Scissors / knives | symbolizes cutting the tie | tea, spirits, a book |
| Green hat | slang for being cuckolded | any other color / item |

Also: no white flowers at weddings, no pears split between friends, no clocks to elders.

## 3. Banquet seating (round table)

- **Facing the door = 主位 (host seat).** Most senior person sits here.
- **To the host's right = 主宾 (guest of honor).** Left = 二宾 (second guest).
- **Opposite the host (back to the door) = 副陪** — this person pours, toasts, keeps the table moving.
- **Toast order:** the host opens the first toast; juniors then toast seniors, **clockwise**, never skipping someone.
- **Glass rim etiquette:** a junior raises their glass rim *below* the senior's rim as a sign of respect.

## 4. Bribery boundary

- Etiquette (a modest birthday gift, 随份子 within the table above, shared meals) is normal.
- **Cross the line when:** cash or expensive goods go to a boss, client, or official to *influence* a decision, get a job, or win a contract — especially after a decision is pending. That is a compliance / anti-corruption risk, not politeness.
- **Flag it back to the user** and do not optimize the script. If asked to "gift" an official to secure a deal, decline and note the legal risk.
