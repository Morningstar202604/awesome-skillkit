# Used-Car Minimums

Buying a used car is asymmetric: the seller knows the car's history. You must
demand evidence, not trust.

## Non-negotiable minimums

### 1. Independent third-party inspection

- Pay for an **independent** inspector (¥300–800, money well spent).
- **Do NOT use a mechanic the dealer recommends.** That shop works for the
  dealer, not you.
- Choose an inspection service that is paid by you, reports directly to you,
  and is not on the dealer's referral list.
- The inspection should cover: frame, accident history, flood signs, mechanical
  health, electronics, test drive.

### 2. Demand the records

Before you pay any deposit:

- **出险记录** (insurance claim history) — shows every accident claim filed.
  Request it in writing; the seller can pull it from the insurance system.
- **4S 维保记录** (4S-shop maintenance records) — shows service history,
  mileage consistency, and any warranty repairs.
- If the seller hesitates, stalls, or refuses to show records, **walk away.**
  Legitimate sellers hand these over freely.

### 3. Title status

Confirm the car's title (登记证) is clean:

- Not 抵押 (mortgaged / financed — the bank holds the title).
- Not 查封 (seized / court-ordered).
- The seller on the contract matches the name on the title.
- If there is an outstanding loan, require it to be paid off and the mortgage
  released **before** money changes hands.

## What to check yourself

### Mileage consistency

- Compare the odometer reading today with the mileage on service records.
- If service records show 80,000 km last year and the odometer reads 40,000 km
  now, the odometer was rolled back. Walk away.

### Accident signs

- Uneven panel gaps left vs right.
- Overspray on plastic trim, rubber seals, or glass.
- Weld marks in the engine bay or trunk (factory welds are uniform; accident
  welds look rough).
- Seatbelt date code: if the seatbelt was replaced after the car's build date,
  the car may have been in an accident severe enough to deploy belt pretensioners.
- Mismatched paint under angled light.
- Frame rails with straightening marks or kinks.

### Flood / water-damage signs

- Musty, damp smell inside (air freshener is a cover-up).
- Mud or sand under the seats, in seat rails, under the dashboard.
- Rust in unusual places (inside the trunk, on seat springs, under the dash).
- Corroded electrical connectors, flickering lights, weird electronic glitches.
- Water line on the door jamb or under the dashboard.

## Test drive (at least 20 minutes, on mixed roads)

- **Cold start:** start the engine cold (first thing in the morning is ideal).
  Smoke on cold start can be an issue.
- **Transmission:** shifts should be smooth, no clunks or slipping.
- **Brakes:** at moderate speed, brake firmly — the car should pull straight,
  not to one side.
- **Steering:** no vibration through the wheel at speed, no pulling.
- **Electronics:** every window, lock, mirror, AC, radio, backup camera,
  sensors.
- **Road noise:** listen for odd clunks over bumps (suspension / bushings).

## Walk-away signals

- Seller refuses to show 出险记录 or 4S 维保记录.
- Seller refuses an independent inspection.
- Title shows 抵押 or 查封 and seller will not clear it.
- Price is dramatically below market — there is a reason.
- Pressure to decide "today" / "another buyer is coming."
- VIN does not match across dashboard, door jamb, and title.

## Price reality

Used-car pricing is transparent on public platforms. Compare the same year,
trim, and mileage across at least 5 listings. If a car is 20% cheaper than the
market, there is a hidden reason.
