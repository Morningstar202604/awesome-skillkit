# Delivery Inspection (提车) Checklist

Do this **on the delivery lot, before signing the handover form, before driving
off.** Take photos of everything. Bring a friend who knows cars if possible.
Most importantly: check the **date codes** — they catch swapped tires, accident
repairs, and rebuilt cars.

## 1. Build date on the door jamb

- Open the driver's door; look for a sticker on the door jamb or B-pillar with
  the build date.
- Must be recent (within 3 months domestic, 6 months imported — see
  price-negotiation.md on inventory cars).
- If older, negotiate or refuse.

## 2. Tire date code (DOT code)

On the tire sidewall, find a 4-digit code starting with "DOT". The last four
digits are the week and year:

```
DOT XXXX 2324
           ^^  ^^
           |   +-- 2024
           +------ week 23 (early June 2024)
```

- **All four tires** should have date codes **before the car's build date**
  (tires are mounted after the car is built).
- If any tire's date code is **later** than the car's build date, that tire was
  swapped. Ask why. Walk away if the answer does not add up.
- Tire age also matters: tires older than 5 years (even with tread) are
  degraded.

## 3. Glass date codes

Every window has a date code (usually dots before/after a number, e.g. `...4`
= first half of 2024, `.4` = second half; exact format varies by manufacturer).

- **Every piece of glass** must be dated **before or at** the car's build date.
- If any window (windshield, side, rear) is dated **later** than the car build
  date, that glass was replaced — a sign of accident repair. Walk away or
  demand an explanation in writing.
- Windshield replacement alone can be from a stone chip, but side/rear glass
  replacement is a bigger red flag.

## 4. Engine-bay screws and torque marks

- Look at the visible bolts on the engine cover, strut towers, and frame rails.
- Factory bolts have uniform torque-paint marks (a dab of colored paint across
  the bolt and the surface it sits in).
- If marks are missing, misaligned, or different colors across bolts, the
  engine bay has been disassembled. Ask why.

## 5. Panel gaps and paint

- Walk around the car in bright daylight.
- Panel gaps (between hood and fenders, doors and fenders, trunk and quarters)
  should be even and consistent left-to-right.
- Look for orange-peel texture, overspray on plastic trim, or mismatched paint
  under angled light — signs of a repaint.

## 6. Electronics and accessories

- Test every power window, door lock, mirror adjustment, seat heater/cooler.
- Test air conditioning: cold air comes out within a minute.
- Test radio, Bluetooth, speakers, backup camera, parking sensors.
- Turn on every light: headlights (high/low), turn signals, brake lights,
  fog lights, interior lights, trunk light.
- Check infotainment pairs with your phone.

## 7. Mileage and supplies

- Mileage on the odometer should be **under 50 km** for a new car (a few km
  from factory transport and PDI).
- Confirm the trunk has: spare tire (or repair kit), jack, lug wrench,
  triangular warning sign, reflective vest, towing hook, owner's manual,
  warranty booklet, two keys.

## 8. VIN match

- The VIN on the dashboard (visible through the windshield) must match:
  - the VIN on the door jamb sticker
  - the VIN on the contract and invoice
  - the VIN on the warranty booklet
  - the VIN on the engine bay (if stamped)
- If any do not match, stop and escalate.

## 9. Paperwork before you drive off

- Invoice (发票) in your name and ID number
- Contract showing the bare-car price you agreed on
- Warranty booklet stamped
- Temporary plate (临时牌照) if permanent plates are pending
- Insurance policy (交强险 mandatory, plus commercial)
- All keys and remotes

Do not sign the "car accepted, no further issues" form until every item above is
checked. Take photos of the car and all paperwork.
