# Sources and Licenses

This skill compiles general consumer-protection guidance for car buyers and
owners in China. It does **not** provide mechanical diagnosis, insurance
advice, or investment advice.

## Source categories

- **China Consumers' Association (中国消费者协会) car-buying reports,
  2025–2026** — public reports on common dealer complaints: bundled discounts,
  inventory-car sales, mandatory decoration packages, loan markups, and
  delivery-dispute patterns.
- **Major automaker owner's manuals (publicly available)** — maintenance
  intervals for oil, filters, brake fluid, coolant, spark plugs, transmission
  fluid, timing belts/chains. These are publicly published PDFs on manufacturer
  websites; intervals are generalized across brands in this skill.
- **Used-car industry best practices** — third-party inspection norms, the
  importance of 出险记录 (insurance claim history) and 4S 维保记录 (maintenance
  records), and walk-away signals. These reflect widely accepted consumer
  practice rather than a single standard.
- **Date-code inspection guidance** — general consumer-knowledge on reading DOT
  tire codes, glass date codes, and door-jamb build dates; widely published in
  automotive consumer media.

## Disclaimer

- Maintenance intervals are general reference values; **the owner's manual for
  the exact year, make, and model is the authority.**
- This skill does not diagnose mechanical problems. For repairs, use a licensed
  mechanic.
- Date-code inspection is a consumer verification step, not an engineering
  assessment; a professional pre-purchase inspection is still recommended,
  especially for used cars.
- Contract language varies by dealer and jurisdiction; this is general guidance,
  not legal advice. For disputes, consult the local consumer association
  (12315) or a lawyer.
