# Sources and Licenses

This skill compiles general patient-communication guidance. It does **not**
contain medical advice and should be used only to prepare for a conversation
with a licensed clinician.

## Source categories

- **National Health Commission (国家卫生健康委员会) outpatient service
  guidelines and patient-facing materials** — general guidance on how Chinese
  outpatient departments are organized, what 挂号 / 导诊台 / 诊室 / 药房
  mean, and expected patient flow.
- **Chinese Medical Association (中华医学会) patient education materials** —
  public pamphlets on preparing for a doctor visit, describing symptoms, and
  questions to ask. These are written for patients and emphasize communication
  rather than treatment.
- **Patient safety literature** — general consensus on the value of written
  symptom summaries, medication lists (including OTC and supplements), and
  post-visit note-taking; widely discussed in health-services research and
  patient-safety literature.
- **Common department name reference** — standard Chinese hospital department
  naming used across public tertiary hospitals.

## Disclaimer

The sources above inform the *behavioral* guidance (how to communicate, what to
bring, what questions to ask). They are not used to give diagnosis, treatment,
or dosing advice. The skill repeatedly restates that AI cannot diagnose or
prescribe, and that emergencies require calling 120.

For any specific medical question, see a licensed clinician in person.
